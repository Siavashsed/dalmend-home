**Answer this question:** What exactly did the NAR settlement in 2024 change about real estate agent commissions?

The 2024 NAR settlement fundamentally restructured how commissions are negotiated, though most people misunderstand what actually changed.

Here's what it did: The settlement eliminated the practice of automatically posting buyer agent commissions on the Multiple Listing Service (MLS) as part of the seller's listing agreement. Previously, sellers essentially agreed upfront to pay a commission that would compensate both the listing agent and the buyer's agent. Now, these are separate negotiations.

What it didn't do: Cap commission percentages. The 6% commission (or whatever your market standard is) isn't legally prohibited. However, the structural change means that buyer compensation must now be negotiated directly between the buyer and their agent, rather than being assumed as part of the seller's costs.

The practical impact varies wildly by market. In competitive buyer's markets, I've seen buyers negotiate lower buyer agent commissions. In seller's markets, some agents have maintained similar structures through different framing. The key difference is transparency—everything is now explicitly negotiated rather than baked into the listing.

As someone who's been through multiple transactions since the settlement, the biggest change I've noticed is that commissions are now a conversation starter rather than a hidden line item. This actually gives informed buyers and sellers more leverage.
