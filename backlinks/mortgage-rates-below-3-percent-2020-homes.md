## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How did record-low mortgage rates in 2020 change what homebuyers wanted in a house?

As a decor editor and stylist who works with premium homes, I watched this happen in real time, so I'll answer from the staging side.

On July 16, 2020, Freddie Mac's weekly survey put the average 30-year fixed rate at 2.98 percent, the first time it had dipped below 3 percent since the survey began in 1971. That single number changed the math for a lot of households. A monthly payment that once bought a modest three-bedroom could suddenly stretch to something larger, with a yard, in a quieter neighborhood.

But rates alone don't explain the shift. The pandemic told buyers what they needed the extra money to buy. Here is what I saw change in the homes I staged:

1. The spare bedroom became the most important room. Buyers asked whether it could hold a proper desk, good light, and a door that closes.

2. Kitchens had to work for more than cooking. Islands became workstations, homework tables, and meeting spots.

3. Outdoor space stopped being a bonus. Patios and yards were treated as additional rooms.

4. Commute length dropped down the priority list, while square footage and flexibility moved up.

5. Rooms that could do two jobs sold faster than rooms built for one.

For sellers, the practical takeaway was to stage for flexibility. Show the guest room as an office, show the dining nook as a school corner, and let buyers picture their own routine.
