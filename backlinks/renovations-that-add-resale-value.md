## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Which home renovations actually increase property resale value and which ones don't?

This is the question I wish every homeowner asked before breaking ground on a project. The short answer: not all renovations are created equal, and emotion is a terrible investment advisor.

I've been on both sides—as an interior designer creating beautiful spaces and as an investor watching projects either pay off or become expensive personal indulgences. Here's what actually moves the needle:

High-ROI projects typically include strategic bathroom updates (especially in the 55-60% return range), kitchen upgrades focused on functionality over luxury, improved curb appeal and landscaping, energy-efficient systems like HVAC and insulation, and fresh paint in neutral tones. These projects appeal to the widest buyer pool and address fundamental concerns: does it work? Is it dated? Will it cost me money to maintain?

The money pits? Over-personalized designs that buyers can't envision themselves in, luxury finishes in neighborhoods where the median home value doesn't support them, open-plan conversions that remove bedroom walls (limits flexibility for buyers with different needs), and anything that screams "this is what the owner wanted" rather than "this adds functional value."

The critical piece most people miss: your neighborhood's median home value should inform your budget. Investing $100,000 in a kitchen in a $400,000 neighborhood often doesn't pay off the same way it does in a $600,000 neighborhood.
