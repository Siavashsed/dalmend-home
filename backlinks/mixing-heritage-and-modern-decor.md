## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How do you mix antique or heritage furniture with modern decor without the room looking cluttered or mismatched?

The short answer is to use a ratio instead of relying on taste. Let one era lead at roughly 70 percent and give the other the remaining 30. Most rooms that feel off are not suffering from bad pieces. They are suffering from two styles demanding equal airtime.

Here is how I approach it as a stylist.

First, choose the lead. Look at the architecture. A 1920s Georgian-style home, for instance, already speaks heritage, so it makes sense for traditional pieces to carry the room and for modern items to play the supporting role. A glass-and-steel loft would flip that.

Second, pick your anchors. Last March I worked in a Charlotte living room where the owners had a walnut secretary desk and a bone-white Roche Bobois sofa. Both were strong. Once the desk and the architecture led, the sofa stopped competing and started contrasting, which is exactly what you want from the 30 percent.

Third, create a bridge. Repeat one material, color or finish across both eras. A warm wood tone in a modern side table, or a clean-lined lamp with a brass base that echoes older hardware, will make the pairing feel deliberate.

Fourth, edit. When you mix eras, negative space does a lot of work. Give each statement piece room so it can be seen on its own terms instead of crowding its neighbor.

Finally, test the room in different light. Pieces that look balanced at noon can shift in the gray light of early morning, so check before you commit to final placement.
