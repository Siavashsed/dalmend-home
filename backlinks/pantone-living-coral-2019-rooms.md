## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Is Pantone's Living Coral a good color to paint an entire room?

In most rooms, no. That isn't because Living Coral is a bad color. It's a beautiful one, warm and full of life, and it deserves a place in your home. It just works best in small, deliberate doses rather than on all four walls.

I learned this the expensive way. This February a client wanted her powder room done entirely in Living Coral. We had the paint store scan the Pantone chip for an exact match, and by 4 p.m. on the first afternoon the north light had turned the walls into a shade none of us expected. Two things went wrong at once.

First, a strong, saturated color on every wall bounces light back onto itself. The room looks far more intense than the chip, especially in a small space like a powder room or a hallway.

Second, the direction of the light changes everything. Cool north light pulls a warm coral toward something duller and heavier. A south facing room will treat it more kindly, but it can still look loud at midday.

Here is what I recommend instead:

1. Use coral where it can be the accent. Try a front door, a lacquered vanity, an upholstered chair, pillows and throws, or one strong piece of art.
2. Pair it with warm whites, soft greige, navy or natural wood so it has room to breathe.
3. If you still want it on the walls, paint a sample board at least two feet square. Move it around the room and look at it morning, afternoon and evening before you buy a gallon.
4. Think about a single feature wall in a room with good southern or western light.

I wrote up the whole powder room story, with the pairings that ended up working, here: https://interiors.dalmend.com/pantone-living-coral-2019-rooms/

In short, love the color and use it on purpose. Just don't let it take over the whole room.
