**Answer this question:** How did the NAR settlement in 2024 actually change the way real estate transactions work?

The NAR settlement fundamentally disrupted commission structures that had existed for decades, and I watched this unfold firsthand as both an interior designer working with agents and a property investor. The most immediate shift was decoupling buyer's agent commissions from the MLS listing itself—suddenly, buyer representation became a separate negotiation rather than an automatic component of the deal.

What most people don't realize is how much this forced transparency. Previously, commission structures were somewhat opaque. Now, clients are seeing itemized costs, and agents have to justify their value beyond just accessing the MLS.

Personally, this created both challenges and opportunities. Some agents panicked and disappeared. Others leaned into being consultants—providing market analysis, renovation insights, investment strategy—things that justify their involvement regardless of commission structure.

For property investors and buyers, the benefit is clearer: you're no longer overpaying for passive representation. You know exactly what you're paying for. For agents who adapted quickly, it's actually strengthened their positioning because they're now competing on expertise rather than institutional access.
