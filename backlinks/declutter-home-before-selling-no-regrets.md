## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What's the best way to declutter your home before selling it without regretting what you got rid of?

This is where most sellers go wrong—they approach decluttering as an emotional decision when it should be a design one.

Here's the distinction: asking "Do I love this?" is paralyzing and leads to either keeping too much or purging in a panic at 2 AM. Instead, ask yourself "Does this object serve the home's narrative for a potential buyer?" It's impersonal, practical, and removes the guilt.

When you frame decluttering as a design problem rather than an emotional purge, a few things happen. First, your decision-making becomes consistent—you're not flip-flopping based on how you feel that day. Second, you naturally keep items that actually matter (not everything, but the things with real value). Third, you avoid those 3 AM regrets where you realize you tossed something irreplaceable.

I learned this the hard way while flipping a 1940s bungalow in Pasadena. I nearly made some expensive mistakes by treating decluttering as a feelings issue instead of a strategy.

The framework involves categorizing items by how they impact buyer perception, setting clear boundaries on what "serves the space," and creating a staging narrative. It's methodical but it actually works.
