**Title:** Has anyone else found that lighting did more for a calm room than any furniture?

Something I keep noticing when I help friends and clients rework their living rooms: the rooms that feel calm almost never got there through the furniture. They got there through the lights.

The usual setup is one bright ceiling fixture doing everything. It flattens the room, throws shadows under your eyes, and makes the space feel like it is waiting for you to do something. You can buy linen, wood and ceramics all day and the room still reads as tense under that light.

What actually shifted things for me:

- Turning the overhead off after sunset and living on lamps
- Keeping bulbs warm, around 2700K, with one color temperature per room
- Putting light at three heights: a floor lamp, a table lamp, and something low like a shelf lamp
- Adding dimmers to anything that stays overhead

It costs a fraction of a new sofa and changes the room more. Curious whether others have found the same, or if anyone has a room where the overhead light genuinely works.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why doesn't my living room feel calm even after I redecorated it?

In most homes, what makes a room feel unsettled is not the decor. It is the light.

I'm a decor editor and stylist, and I see this all the time. People invest in soft textiles, natural materials and handmade pieces, and the room still feels tense. Last February I worked on a townhouse where the client had spent roughly $18,000 on linen slipcovers, a travertine coffee table and a shelf of hand-thrown ceramics, and her living room still did not feel calm to her. The objects were lovely. The single bright ceiling fixture was undoing all of them.

Overhead light works against calm in a few ways. It lights everything evenly, so nothing recedes and the eye never rests. It flattens texture, and texture is the main reason you buy linen or stone in the first place. It also keeps the room in daytime mode long after you want to wind down.

Here is what I would try before buying anything else:

1. Stop using the ceiling light in the evening. Live on lamps for a week and notice the difference.
2. Light at three heights: a floor lamp, a table lamp, and something low, like a small lamp on a shelf.
3. Use warm bulbs, around 2700K, and keep one color temperature per room. Mixed whites read as visual noise.
4. Put a dimmer on any overhead fixture you keep.
5. Aim light at surfaces, such as a wall, a curtain or a textured object, rather than straight down onto the floor.

None of this is expensive, and it often does more than a new sofa would. Once the light is right, the decor you already own can do its job.
