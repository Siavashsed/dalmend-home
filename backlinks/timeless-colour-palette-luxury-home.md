## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How many colours should I use in a luxury home to keep the look timeless?

Fewer than you think. The rule I give clients is three tones you could genuinely live with for twenty years, plus one that's allowed to change. Everything beyond that is decoration, not foundation.

I learned this the hard way on a Hampstead townhouse I styled in 2019. The owners had picked eleven paint colours across 3,400 square feet. Each looked lovely on its own sample board. But on a rainy 8 a.m. walkthrough, with grey light pouring through the skylights, none of them agreed with each other. Creams went muddy, greys turned cold, and the house read as a patchwork rather than a single home.

Here's how I'd approach it:

1. Choose three base tones: a main wall colour, a deeper secondary for joinery or feature areas, and a neutral for ceilings and trim. These should work together in every room.

2. Keep one flexible tone. This is your accent, and it can shift with taste or season through cushions, throws, art or a single painted wall.

3. Test in your worst light. Look at samples on a dull morning and in the evening under artificial light, not just at noon on a sunny day.

4. Let texture do the work. Linen, stone, oak, wool, brass and plaster add depth without adding another paint colour.

5. Repeat, don't introduce. When a colour appears in three places, it feels intentional. When it appears once, it feels like a mistake.
