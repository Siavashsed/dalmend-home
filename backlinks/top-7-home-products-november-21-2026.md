## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Are cordless rechargeable table lamps actually worth buying for your home?

Short answer: yes, in most homes, and not for the reason people assume.

I'm a decor editor and stylist, and I've placed a lot of lamps in a lot of rooms. The common pitch for cordless lamps is convenience, and that's true. But the bigger benefit is placement. A corded lamp has to live near an outlet, so you end up with light where the wiring is, not where the room needs it. A rechargeable lamp lets you put light on a dining table, a window ledge, a bookshelf, a patio table, or a bedside spot where there's no socket within reach.

I learned this on a job last March. I spent forty minutes in a Brooklyn dining room trying to hide an extension cord behind a console. Two weeks later I used a cordless lamp in the same space and the problem simply went away.

Here's what I'd check before you buy:

1. Battery life at real brightness. Manufacturers quote the best-case number on the lowest setting. Look for honest figures at medium brightness.
2. Dimming and color temperature. Warm light in the 2200K to 2700K range makes evening rooms feel calm. A good dimmer matters more than a high maximum output.
3. Charging. USB-C is easier to live with than a proprietary dock, and you can use chargers you already own.
4. Stability. A cordless lamp gets moved around, so the base should be weighted and the finish durable.
5. Build quality. Cheap hinges, switches, and shades are what fail first.

Where they don't make sense: a primary reading lamp you use for hours every night is still better on a cord, since you'll recharge less. For accent, dining, and entertaining light, cordless wins.
