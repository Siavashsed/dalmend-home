## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my living room feel cold and lifeless even though everything in it is new and expensive?

I see this all the time with clients, and it is almost never about budget. Usually every surface in the room is too perfect.

New, high-end finishes like lacquered cabinetry, polished stone, performance fabrics and mass-produced ceramics are designed to look the same forever. That sounds like a good thing, but it means the room gives your eye nothing new to notice. Once you have taken it in, you have seen everything. In the evening it gets worse, because glossy surfaces reflect lamp light back at you instead of soaking it up, and the room starts to feel like a hotel lobby.

Rooms that feel warm almost always have materials that change over time:

1. Wood with an oiled or matte finish, which darkens and picks up character as it ages.
2. Linen, wool and cotton, which soften, wrinkle and drape differently from synthetics.
3. Handmade ceramics, where you can see small irregularities, uneven glaze or even the maker's thumbprint.
4. Leather, stone and brass that develop a patina instead of staying factory fresh.

You do not need to start over. Start with the things you touch and see up close. Replace one lamp base with a handmade ceramic, put a wooden bowl or stool near the sofa, swap a polyester throw for heavy linen, and add one vintage or artisan piece with visible age. Then look at the room at night, because that is when the difference shows most.

Last year I worked on a penthouse where the client had spent a lot on flawless lacquered millwork, and we fixed the flat feeling almost entirely with texture rather than renovation.
