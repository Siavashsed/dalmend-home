## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How should I decorate my dining table for everyday use without it getting in the way of actually eating there?

Start by taking things away. Most everyday tables fail because they have too much on them, not too little.

The rule I use with clients is that you should be able to clear the entire table in under two minutes. That covers everything you would need to move to serve a meal, wipe the surface down and put it back. If it takes longer, the table is styled for a photograph, and over time you will start avoiding it. I have seen this happen in very beautiful homes. Last March I styled a Georgian-style dining room in Connecticut where the owner admitted she had eaten standing at her kitchen island for eleven months, because her table looked too finished to touch.

Here is what works on a table you actually use:

1. One soft base layer. A linen runner suits most tables. It adds texture, protects the wood and lifts off in seconds.

2. Two or three small objects at most. Choose low pieces you can move with one hand, so they never block anyone's view across the table.

3. Put the money into what you touch. One heavy linen napkin per setting makes a weeknight dinner feel deliberate in a way a large centerpiece never will. Good linen also gets better with washing, which matters more for daily use than for a dinner party.

4. Leave empty space on purpose. A bare surface reads as calm and inviting, not unfinished.

5. Keep anything seasonal or decorative in a nearby basket or sideboard and bring it out when you host.
